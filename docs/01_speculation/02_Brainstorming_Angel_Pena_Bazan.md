**Role-Shift Brainstorming**

From the Perspective of a First Time Participant

* First time players will encounter their first hurdles as they get used to the base rules of Revolt 86\. Artifice Trackers are external tile trackers used to place traps. They will have a hard time keeping track of these Artifice Trackers throughout the game. Knowing when to place each of the three different types of cards throughout their turn is also a learning curve without the smart board and hopefully could be coded into the smart board display. Players assume that the smart board will carry most of the responsibility of the player during each turn. But without the basic understanding of the rules, new players will struggle to keep up as the smart board is only one part of the immersive experience that bridges this board game to our world. 

**Extreme Constraint** 

* What remains when most resources are removed? 


With most of the resources removed (Displays, Bluetooth die, Card Readers, LED Board, RFID Stickers.), what remains is a tightly designed board game that has a high fidelity 3D printed board and pieces with printed trading cards and an overall polished experience. 

* What absolutely cannot be cut?

The board itself cannot be cut, it has to be a well designed board with a grid of 8 x 12\.  
As well as game pieces and cards.

* What does the project become at its smallest possible scale?

At its smallest possible scale it becomes only a concept and a low fidelity makeshift board game.

**Inspirational Research**

Chess comes to mind as a huge inspiration for the layout of my board and pieces as they are both set up very similarly, explicitly noting a strong resemblance in the position of each piece on their respective board. Although the board for Revolt 86 is a different grid and its pieces have different moves, both of these games are turn-based. I truly enjoy the simplicity of chess and its vast amount of outcomes created by its game mechanics. This creates an environment for competition and exciting strategic plays. My game will take this environment and multiply its outcomes as new game mechanics are being used. The goal is to have each player come up with the best possible move from whatever ability and dice roll they're given at random. I want a game that keeps you on the edge of your seat. I want players to find moves and different ways to keep their lead or gain the upper hand. I took inspiration from a chessboard layout and since chess already has a distinct game experience I will not be translating anything else to my project. 

Another inspiration is a smart chess board, only for its hardware and the proof of concept that my smart board can be created. It gives me a starting point from where to start and what it would take to market it. I would like to put my game out there for other people to enjoy. 

As for the robot mechs and crazy futuristic war machines, the inspiration came from a first person shooter called Titanfall 2\. This was a game where you could ride a variety of different mechs with different abilities. I really enjoyed the combat between players, a fast pace combat  using a variety of abilities and firearms. So I wanted a way to implement a similar combat style to fit a physical board with a grid. I loved Titanfall 2’s sci-fi storytelling and its environment so I created my own war-ridden future.   
**Deliverable**

There are a lot of recurring questions related to the computing part of the project. The biggest question was if it was even  possible for every component to communicate with the microcomputer inside the physical board. There is a real strong interest on the design side of the game such as the board, art work for cards, and the design for the game’s physical pieces. There is an assumption that all of the cards and mechanics of the game can be translated into code for the board to understand. Need more research on the hardware needed as well as the code needed to create the game logic. I am very surprised that even without a smart board, this game can hold up its own and it will be the start of what could be a bridge between the digital and physical mechanics that will potentially create future smart board games.
